NOL8 · Pre/Post-Inference Control Benchmark

Keep known secrets out of the model, and out of the response.

A model boundary has two edges: what reaches the model, and what leaves it. NOL8 deterministically redacts and masks the known sensitive values at both edges, so the model and the vendor see only safe content and the response ships with secrets removed. It is exact, verified correct against an independent oracle on all 53 prompts, and byte-for-byte identical to Aergia, our stand-up of Google RE2. It also emits route and block signals a control plane can act on, with native enforcement on the roadmap.

03 · What it means

What it means for the model boundary.

›
Secrets never reach the model, or the response.

NOL8 redacts and masks known values at both edges: 25 secrets stripped across the run before the model saw them or the response shipped. The model, the vendor, and the user each receive only what they should. Prompt tokens to the model fall 613 to 562, output tokens released 566 to 537.

›
Byte-identical to the incumbent, provably.

Themis and Aergia, our stand-up of Google RE2, produced identical output on all 53 prompts at both edges, and both match an independent oracle. As correct as the established engine, running inline on FPGA.

›
NOL8 acts on the data; the control plane enforces.

Redact and mask are live today: NOL8 transforms the prompt and the output itself. Route and block are signals NOL8 emits for a control plane to act on. NOL8 does not stop a prompt reaching the model, or withhold an output, today; native enforcement is on the roadmap, and the report marks it as such.

›
Known values, today.

NOL8 governs exact literals. Matching is substring-exact, so a value can over-trigger on a longer phrase that contains it, and a masked form is capped at 15 characters. Word-boundary patterns and classification are the complementary layer, on the roadmap.

Method
Corpus
53 synthetic prompts shaped like traffic around a model boundary, each with a deterministic model-stub profile. No prompt is stopped: NOL8 redacts or masks it and the model is always called.
Policy
One literal boundary policy (demos/policies/boundary.nol) plus an action map (boundary-actions.json), both generated by build_boundary_policy.py: internal projects and accounts redacted, cards masked to last four, privileged-output markers redacted, flagged entities and blocked phrases signalled. The same file runs on both engines.
Actions
LIVE (NOL8 transforms the data): redact (value -> [REDACT]), mask (card -> XXXX <last4>). ROADMAP (NOL8 emits a signal; a control plane enforces): route (-> [ROUTE]), block (-> [BLOCK]). Replacements are capped at 15 characters by ISSUE-005, which is why the mask is compact.
Engines
Themis, the NOL8 FPGA (:443), and Aergia, our stand-up of Google RE2 (:444). Each is called at both control points: once on the prompt, once on the model output. NOL8 does not route, block, or stop a message today; it emits the signal and the redacted text flows on.
Oracle
The framework Aho-Corasick matcher independently computes the correct leftmost-longest replacement at each edge over boundary.nol. Themis and Aergia both reproduce it, 53 of 53 prompts.
Parity
Themis and Aergia output compared per prompt at both edges: identical across all 53. Same policy, same data, same result.
Model
A deterministic stub stands in for the model, so the benchmark measures the controls, not model quality.

Not a model-quality or throughput test. A deterministic stub isolates the boundary controls. Route and block are roadmap signals, marked as such.
